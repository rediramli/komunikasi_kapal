From Model to Metal — Measuring the Split Decisions on the Real Link

29 October 2026 · Live testbed (real cellular SIMs) · greedy in-order goodput via the pinned sub-stream sender

The win-table was model-based. Here we measure the key decisions on the real bonded link: we drive a saturating stream through the actual per-SIM pinned sub-streams at different split weights w, with and without the S4 impairment, and record the real in-order goodput (what a video actually gets) and stall fraction.

What the live run proves

  • Under S4, the smart decision wins by a landslide. The fixed static split (w=0.5) sends half the video down the degraded Simba path; in-order reassembly then stalls completely — no video gets through. The AI/rule decision (w=0, all on healthy Singtel) delivers 35.6 Mbit/s. This is the decision the controller makes, measured on the real link.
  • Even with both links healthy, naive 50/50 bonding is not the best. Leaning to the faster SIM (35.7) beats the balanced split (29.2) because splitting one in-order stream across asymmetric paths causes head-of-line stalls. The right split depends on the live asymmetry — exactly what a context-aware controller is for.

Honest refinement to the model

The offline model treated the reorder penalty as mostly latency and assumed goodput peaks at a balanced split. The live link shows the reorder penalty also cuts in-order goodput (bonded 29.2 < Singtel-alone 35.7; and total collapse under S4). So on the real link the case for a smart split is stronger than the model implied, not weaker. We will recalibrate the model's reorder term to this measured data — and note that stall fractions are high across the board (0.73–1.0), i.e. in-order video delivery over bonded cellular is genuinely hard and worth optimising.

Method

Pinned sub-stream sender (mpproxy_send.py, N=8: 4 per SIM, source-bound to each WAN IP) to a reassembling receiver on the VPS (mpproxy_recv.py), greedy (saturating), 7–8 s per probe. S4 applied with the held netem injector. Metric = receiver-reported in-order goodput and stall fraction. Current live asymmetry: Singtel markedly faster than Simba (35 vs 15 Mbit/s alone), so "lean to Singtel" was right even when clean — a different day's asymmetry would flip which SIM to favour, which is the point.
