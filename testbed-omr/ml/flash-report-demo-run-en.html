The Testbed Under Maritime Conditions — Static Walk & Dynamic Voyage

29 October 2026 · One continuous ~4:45 run · recorded from the live dashboard · Jetson LinUCB edge AI

This is the run we demonstrated: one SIM (Simba) is pushed through a sequence of real maritime conditions while Singtel stays healthy, and the edge AI's live decision is recorded throughout. It has two parts — a static walk through each condition, then a continuous dynamic voyage.

Part 1 — Static walk (S0 → S6)

Each condition is held steady for ~25 s, so we can see exactly how the AI responds to one well-defined impairment at a time. The conditions are calibrated to the real PSA Agility traces.

Reading left to right: as the impairment deepens, Simba's RTT climbs. The AI moves the load off Simba (w→0) exactly on the packet-loss phases (S2, S4), then recovers. The stand-out is S6: 600 ms of pure latency with no loss — and the AI deliberately keeps the load, because high latency alone does not justify abandoning a SIM. That is the explainable trade-off, visible live.

Part 2 — Dynamic voyage B

The second half is not a set of steady steps but a continuous "leaving-port" voyage: the ship sails out of good coverage, through congestion and the cell edge, then regains coverage — S0 → S2 → S3 → S4 → S3 → S1 → S0, each phase flowing into the next.

Here the AI is not reacting to a single held condition but tracking a moving target. Simba's RTT rises and falls as the voyage progresses; the AI pushes load to Singtel through the worst of it (the S4 cell edge, mid-voyage) and hands it back as coverage returns at the end — all automatically, decision by decision, 1–2 s at a time. This is the realistic case: conditions never hold still at sea.

What the run shows

  • Failover works, live: load leaves a degrading SIM and returns on recovery, no human action.
  • Explainable, not twitchy: the AI sheds on packet loss (S2, S4) but tolerates pure latency (S6) — matching its goodput-minus-latency objective.
  • Static vs dynamic: the static walk isolates the response to each condition; the voyage proves it holds up under continuously changing, realistic conditions.

Method

One SIM (Simba / wan1) was driven through the conditions with tc netem, each phase held by re-asserting every 1 s (so OMR's path tracker cannot wipe it); Singtel stayed healthy as the reference. The AI's live decision was sampled every 2 s from the Jetson. The whole run auto-restored on completion. Conditions are calibrated to the PSA Agility drive-test ranges.
